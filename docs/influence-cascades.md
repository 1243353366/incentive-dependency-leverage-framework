# How a group traps itself, and where the trap is load-bearing

Five mechanics in causal order, then a layer that runs underneath them. The chain explains how a
position nobody privately chose becomes one nobody can abandon. The layer explains why participation
still feels honest to everyone inside it.

Confidence marks are attached to each claim, because a framework that borrows science to sound
settled is doing the thing it exists to describe.

| # | Mechanic | Mechanism | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Preference falsification** | Public dissent costs more immediately than it pays eventually, so people say the safe thing. Private belief and public signal separate. | Strong (Kuran 1995) |
| 2 | **Reputational cascade** | Only public signals are visible, so apparent consensus is read as information by the next person, who also complies | Moderate to strong (Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch 1992) |
| 3 | **Audience costs** | Once a position is public, retreat is read as capitulation rather than correction | **Contested.** The international-relations tests (Snyder and Borghard 2011) found the costs small, rare, and often absent |
| 4 | **Escalation of commitment** | Sunk cost plus loss aversion make backing out feel like booking a total loss rather than cutting one | Strong (Staw 1976) |
| 5 | **Commitment device** | Rules, contracts and institutions pension the position in so future-you cannot unwind it | Strong (Elster 1979) |

Read down the column, not across the rows: 1 hides the doubt, 2 manufactures the consensus, 3
penalises the defection, 4 pays for the continuation, 5 removes the exit. Nothing in that sequence
requires an order to be given.

## The layer: conduct is dual by default

Every act in a cascade is sincere to the person doing it and instrumental in the record it leaves.
Neither party has to be lying.

The petty insult was genuinely felt *and* produced a timestamp. The complaint was genuine
risk-routing *and* leverage. That duality is not a bug in the framework; it is why the framework
needs no villain to work. It is also why "I only reported what they actually did" and "they only lost
their temper" can both be true at once, and why a reviewing body can find against the actor while
discounting the complainant in the same file.

### Two ledgers, judged separately

| Ledger | Question | What is excluded |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actor's | Was the act voluntary, and did it breach the standard? | Motive, provocation, how understandable the outburst was |
| Designer's | Was the environment built so that breach was the predictable path? | The genuineness of the grievance that started it |

The trap works while one ledger is clean. It fails when the other one fills: evidence of
encouragement turns the complainant's own conduct dual, and their opponent's conduct into
provocation. This is the clean-hands problem, and it arrives by discovery rather than by argument.

## What the evidence actually supports

- Cascades among rational people: **supported**. Informational cascade models produce stampedes into
  positions nobody privately holds.
- Conformity over character: **supported, in a corrective direction**. The anonymity literature's
  "anonymity breeds savagery" finding did not survive meta-analysis; anonymity shifts people toward
  the local norm, which can be just as fatal when the norm is to stay quiet.
- Sanctions as calibrated deterrence: **weak**. Punishment severity is set by the punisher's
  reputational needs, and punishment escalates accordingly.
- Audience costs as the locking mechanism: **contested**, and treated here as a design assumption
  rather than a finding.

## Where the trap does and does not hold

It holds where the shadow of the future is long: repeated interaction, persistent identity, cheap
information about who did what, and no easy exit. Village credit networks, commons governance, early
reputation systems.

It decays where identities are disposable and the horizon is short. An account that can be abandoned
in ten seconds cannot be held to anything, by anyone, including a board.

## The boundary, as three questions

Not a lecture; three questions with checkable answers.

1. **Intent.** Is the desired outcome the other party's compliance with a standard, or their damage?
2. **Information.** Do they know the loop they are in, or is the asymmetry the instrument?
3. **Exit.** Is there an off-ramp they can actually take, or only one that costs more than continuing?

A design that answers "standard", "known" and "yes" is enforcement. One that answers "damage",
"hidden" and "no" is a cage with better paperwork.

## The guardrail this document is bound by

The premise of this repository applies here without modification: **my wins should never create a
future problem for me.** A trap that closes on someone whose entire income is their reputation
creates a permanent future-friction owner, and that owner has the strongest available incentive to
reopen an unresolved public record and read the encouragement as the story. Asymmetric leverage does
not stay asymmetric once the record is dual.

That is not a moral objection. It is the same asymmetry, pointed back.
